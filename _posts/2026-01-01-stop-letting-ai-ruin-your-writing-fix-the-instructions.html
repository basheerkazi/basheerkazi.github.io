Let’s get one thing straight.

AI is not the reason most online writing sounds dull, bloated, and robotic.
Bad instructions are.

When people say “AI writing feels fake,” what they really mean is this:
It feels over-polished, over-explained, and oddly empty.

That’s not an AI problem.
That’s a briefing problem.

If you tell an AI, “Write a professional article,” it will do exactly what it thinks professionals want. Big words. Safe opinions. Long sentences that say very little.

If you want human writing, you need to ask for it properly.

First: Tell AI What to Avoid

AI defaults to habits that instantly kill clarity.

So block them upfront:

  • No buzzwords or press-release language
  • No filler phrases or exaggerated importance
  • No meta talk like “in this article we will discuss”
  • No “in conclusion” summaries

Constraints don’t limit AI. They guide it.

Second: Define a Real Voice

“Professional tone” is vague.
“Write like a calm expert explaining something to a colleague” is not.

AI writes better when you describe a person, not a mood.

Whether you’re a leader, educator, or founder, always anchor the tone in a real human role.

Third: Force Simplicity and Clarity

AI loves complexity because complexity sounds impressive.

Push back:

  • Ask for simple, everyday language
  • Demand active voice
  • Tell it to remove any sentence that adds no real information

Clear communication is not about sounding smart.
It’s about being understood.

Fourth: Demand Specifics

Generic writing is the biggest giveaway of AI-generated content.

Don’t accept:

  • “Improved outcomes”
  • “Enhanced efficiency”
  • “Greater impact”

Instead, ask for real situations, examples, or decisions.
Specific writing feels lived-in. That’s what readers trust.

Fifth: Fix the Rhythm

AI writes evenly. Humans don’t.

Tell it to:

  • Vary sentence length
  • Use short, direct lines when needed
  • Avoid perfectly smooth flow

If the writing feels too balanced, it usually feels fake.

The Prompt That Actually Works

This is the instruction that changes everything. Copy it. Use it. Share it with your team.

Write in a clear, human, conversational tone.
Avoid buzzwords, exaggerated importance, and press-release language.
Do not use meta commentary like “in this section” or “let’s explore.”
Use simple words and active voice.
Vary sentence length naturally.
Take a clear position instead of hedging.
Avoid forced conclusions and summaries.
Be specific with examples or situations.
Write as if explaining to a smart colleague, not trying to impress anyone.
If a sentence adds no real value, remove it.

One prompt. Massive difference.

One Last Thing Most People Miss

Don’t stop at the first draft.

After the AI responds, say this:
“Rewrite this to sound more natural and less polished. Cut anything that feels like filler.”

Editing prompts are where good writing happens.

Final Thought

AI doesn’t replace human writing.
It exposes weak thinking and vague instructions.

If you use AI with clarity and intention, it becomes a tool for focus, not noise.

That’s how you get growth with purpose, not just more content.
